## Installation and Boot Verification

I installed TrueNAS SCALE to the **SanDisk SATA SSD**, configured its administrator account, and successfully booted to TrueNAS.

Read-only inspection helped distinguish the operating-system boot drive from the separate Windows-containing NVMe:

```bash
lsblk -f
efibootmgr -v
```

Results recorded during the session:
- SanDisk SSD was identified as the TrueNAS boot-pool drive.
- Both internal SSDs were detected.
- Toshiba NVMe retained its Windows partitions.
- UEFI entries for Windows and TrueNAS were inspected.
- No data pool was created and the Windows partitions were not erased.

This was a deliberate data-preservation check, not a disk-wiping or migration exercise.

## Restoring and Verifying the Existing Ubuntu Homelab

After the TrueNAS setup session, I returned the Ethernet connection to the existing Ubuntu server and checked that its working environment had not been disrupted.

Read-only checks included:

```bash
lsblk -o NAME,SIZE,MOUNTPOINTS
sudo docker ps --format "table {{.Names}}\t{{.Status}}"
```

Observed:
- The 1 TB primary Immich drive was mounted at `/mnt/photos`.
- The separate 1 TB backup drive was mounted at `/mnt/immich-backup`.
- The Ubuntu OS drive was mounted at `/`.
- Ubuntu was reachable by SSH.
- **Reported healthy at the check:** Immich Server, Immich Machine Learning, Immich PostgreSQL, Immich Redis, Pi-hole, Uptime Kuma, and Portainer.
- **Initially reported `health: starting`:** `opengym-api-1` and `opengym-web-1` during the intermediate check.
- **Later final check:** **all nine Docker containers reported healthy**, including both OpenGym containers and the Immich, Pi-hole, Uptime Kuma, and Portainer components.

**Scope of verification:** The initial and final observations occurred at different points in the same session. The later check supersedes the earlier `health: starting` status. All nine containers reported healthy at the final check; this is a container-health verification, not an independent end-to-end test of every application.
